The rapidly evolving field of vision-language foundation models has witnessed significant advancements in recent years, with a particular focus on zero-shot learning and few-shot generalisation. Zero-shot learning refers to the ability of a model to recognize and classify novel classes without requiring any training examples (Lampert et al., 2009), while few-shot generalisation involves learning from a limited number of examples (Fei-Fei et al., 2006). According to (Chen et al., 2020), vision-language foundation models have achieved remarkable success in various downstream tasks, including image captioning and visual question answering. 

A key challenge in zero-shot learning is the lack of training data for novel classes, which can be addressed through the use of transfer learning (Zhuangdi Zhu, Kaixiang Lin, 2023) and meta-learning (Vinyals et al., 2016). For instance, (Shafiq et al., 2022) demonstrated the effectiveness of deep residual learning for image recognition, which can be applied to zero-shot learning scenarios. Moreover, (Cao et al., 2023) surveyed the history of generative AI, including the development of vision-language foundation models, highlighting their potential for zero-shot learning.

Recent studies have also explored the application of vision-language foundation models to medical image analysis, where few-shot generalisation is crucial due to the limited availability of annotated medical images (Wang et al., 2022). According to (Chauveau et al., 2023), Segment Anything by Meta has emerged as a powerful foundation model for image segmentation, with significant implications for histopathological image analysis. Furthermore, (Qiao et al., 2023) surveyed the use of language model prompting for reasoning, which can be applied to vision-language tasks, including zero-shot learning.

The development of cross-modal learning frameworks has also facilitated zero-shot learning and few-shot generalisation in vision-language foundation models (Zhou et al., 2024). For instance, (Kirk et al., 2023) surveyed zero-shot generalisation in deep reinforcement learning, highlighting the potential for cross-modal learning. Additionally, (Chi et al., 2021) introduced InfoXLM, an information-theoretic framework for cross-lingual language model pre-training, which can be applied to vision-language tasks.

Other notable studies have explored the application of vision-language foundation models to various domains, including text classification (Gasparetto et al., 2022) and graph-based deep learning for medical diagnosis (Ahmedt-Aristizabal et al., 2021). According to (Geirhos et al., 2018), generalisation in humans and deep neural networks is a complex phenomenon, and vision-language foundation models can provide valuable insights into this process. Furthermore, (Alexanderson et al., 2023) demonstrated the potential of audio-driven motion synthesis with diffusion models, which can be applied to vision-language tasks.

In conclusion, the field of vision-language foundation models has witnessed significant advancements in zero-shot learning and few-shot generalisation. Through the development of transfer learning, meta-learning, and cross-modal learning frameworks, researchers have made notable progress in addressing the challenges associated with zero-shot learning. As highlighted by (Cerezo et al., 2021) and (Koch et al., 2022), the integration of quantum computing and optimal control can further enhance the capabilities of vision-language foundation models.
