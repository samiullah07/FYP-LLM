The concept of zero-shot learning and few-shot generalisation in vision-language foundation models has gained significant attention in recent years (Kyu-Hwan Jung, 2023). Foundation models, as defined by Kyu-Hwan Jung (2023), are large-scale pre-trained models that can be fine-tuned for various downstream tasks. According to Laith Alzubaidi et al. (2021), deep learning techniques, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have been widely used for image recognition and object detection tasks. However, these models often require large amounts of labeled data, which can be challenging to obtain, especially in cases where data is scarce (Laith Alzubaidi et al., 2023). Recent studies have explored the use of prompting methods in natural language processing (NLP) to improve few-shot learning capabilities (Pengfei Liu et al., 2022). For instance, Pengfei Liu et al. (2022) provide a comprehensive survey of prompting methods in NLP, highlighting their potential for improving model performance on unseen tasks. Similarly, the use of deep residual learning for image recognition has been shown to improve model performance on few-shot learning tasks (Muhammad Shafiq et al., 2022). However, the application of these techniques to vision-language foundation models is still a relatively underexplored area (Kashif Rasul et al., 2023). The integration of vision and language in foundation models has been shown to improve model performance on various tasks, including object detection and image segmentation (Li Liu et al., 2019; Risheng Wang et al., 2022). For example, Li Liu et al. (2019) provide a survey of deep learning techniques for generic object detection, highlighting the importance of using large-scale pre-trained models. Additionally, the use of diffusion models for audio-driven motion synthesis has been explored in recent studies (Simon Alexanderson et al., 2023). However, the application of these techniques to vision-language foundation models is still in its early stages (Yihan Cao et al., 2023). Despite the progress made in this area, there are still several research gaps that need to be addressed. For instance, the lack of large-scale datasets for vision-language tasks can limit the performance of foundation models (Karan Singhal et al., 2023). Furthermore, the use of large language models for clinical knowledge encoding has been shown to be effective, but the application of these models to vision-language tasks is still underexplored (Wijegunarathna et al., 2023). Overall, the development of vision-language foundation models with zero-shot learning and few-shot generalisation capabilities is an active area of research, with many opportunities for future exploration (Jun Ma et al., 2024).
